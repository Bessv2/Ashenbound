# Art asset pipeline

How third-party art gets into the game, and what is in it now. Charter section 5
applies: record source, licence, intended use and conversion steps for every
asset, and keep reference art (`docs/art/`) apart from runtime assets.

## Rules

1. **Licence first.** Only assets whose licence allows commercial use in a
   closed-source mobile game. CC0 is preferred. The licence file is copied
   next to the assets.
2. **Provenance.** Each pack is listed below with its source repository and the
   exact commit it was taken from.
3. **Only what is used.** Copy the files the game references, not whole packs.
4. **Runtime folder:** `game/assets/third_party/<author>/`. Changes we make
   (recolours) are generated by a script in `tools/art/` and the output sits
   next to the originals, so they can be remade from source.
5. **Mobile budget:** check draw calls with PerfLog after adding art (see
   [DEVICE_TESTING.md](DEVICE_TESTING.md#how-to-read-the-frame-statistics)).
   Repeated level pieces go through `TileGrid` (MultiMesh), not one node per
   piece.

### Changes we made

- **`warden_texture.png`** is the Knight's palette texture recoloured: steel and
  white to blackened iron, gold to ember orange, red to a deeper crimson (the
  charter's Warden: blackened iron with ember accents). Made by
  `tools/art/make_warden_texture.py knight_texture.png warden_texture.png`
  (needs Pillow).
- **See-through walls.** North walls, gates and tall pillars in the crypt are
  drawn with `resources/materials/dungeon_cutout.tres`
  (`resources/shaders/occluder_cutout.gdshader`): the same KayKit texture and
  roughness, plus a dithered hole around the hero when the surface is between
  it and the camera.
- **`cultist_texture.png`** is the skeleton palette texture with the Mage's
  maroon robe and hat turned black-violet. Made by
  `tools/art/make_cultist_texture.py skeleton_texture.png cultist_texture.png`
  (needs Pillow).
- **No mesh edits.** Hiding parts (the Knight carries every weapon and shield),
  swapping materials and holding a pose are done at runtime by `ModelDressing`
  (`game/scripts/presentation/model_dressing.gd`).

### Animations

Both character models come rigged, with 76 clips (Knight) and 95 (Skeleton Warrior): idle, run,
one- and two-handed attacks, dodges, block, spellcasts, hits, deaths, and
skeleton spawn and dormant poses. `CharacterAnimator` plays them from gameplay
signals; which clip each action uses is set on the Animator node in
`warden.tscn` and `skeleton_warrior.tscn`.

## Measured cost

Rendered main scene on a desktop with software Vulkan (only the counts are
meaningful, not the frame rate):

| | Before the art pass | After |
|---|---|---|
| Draw calls (main scene, 3 targets, 1 Warden) | about 106 | about 192 |
| Web download (`index.pck`, gzipped) | about 0.2 MB | about 4.1 MB |

Each KayKit character is 6 to 10 separate skinned meshes, drawn again for
shadows; that is most of the increase. If a phone struggles, the first steps
are to merge each character's meshes and to turn off shadows on small parts.

## Sound and music

The sound effects and music (CC0, from Kenney and OpenGameArt) follow the same
rules. They are listed with their sources in [AUDIO.md](AUDIO.md#the-files-and-where-they-come-from)
and in `game/assets/third_party/audio/packs.json`. They are rebuilt from the
original downloads by `tools/audio/import_audio.py`.
